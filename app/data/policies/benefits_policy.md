# Benefits and Eligibility Policy

## Eligibility
Benefits coverage begins on the first day of the month following hire for eligible full-time employees. Contractors are not eligible for core health, dental, or retirement benefits unless otherwise specified in their contract.

## Enrollment
Employees must submit benefits elections during the open enrollment window or within 30 days of eligibility. Changes directly related to life events may require supporting documentation.

## Benefits Review
If an employee's benefits status is pending or inactive, HR should review the employee record before advising a candidate on plan eligibility. Benefits guidance must be based on the current employee record and not on outdated policy summaries.

## Plan Information and Decision Ownership
The benefits administrator publishes current plan summaries, enrollment dates, contribution information, and contact channels. Employees should use documents for the plan year in which coverage is requested. A prior-year summary may describe a discontinued option or different cost share and should not be used to make a current election. The assistant may point to the relevant policy section but cannot interpret a plan contract or promise coverage for a particular service. Questions about claim payment, provider networks, or a specific medical service should be directed to the plan administrator.

Plan eligibility is determined from the employee record, employment terms, applicable plan documents, and any location-specific rule. The employee's manager does not decide benefit eligibility. If employment type, start date, leave status, or work location is missing or inconsistent, People Operations should reconcile the record before eligibility guidance is given. A pending status is not a final denial or approval. The employee should receive a clear explanation of what information remains outstanding and where to submit it securely.

## Enrollment and Qualifying Changes
Newly eligible employees should review plan options and submit elections through the approved benefits portal within the published enrollment period. The illustrative window in this corpus is 30 days after eligibility; a current plan notice may set a different deadline. The system should display confirmation after submission. Employees should retain the confirmation number and review the effective date shown in the portal. A saved draft is not necessarily a submitted election.

During annual open enrollment, employees should compare current coverage with next-year choices and verify dependent details. The employee is responsible for submitting elections before the announced close time. If the portal is unavailable near a deadline, the employee should contact the benefits team through the published support channel and retain evidence of the issue. Sensitive dependent or identity documents must not be sent to an individual manager.

Certain life events may permit an election change outside open enrollment. Examples can include marriage, birth or adoption, loss of other coverage, or a qualifying change in work status. The administrator determines which event qualifies, which evidence is required, and how long the employee has to act. Employees should report the event promptly and should not assume that a change will apply retroactively. Documentation should be submitted using the restricted benefits workflow and limited to what the administrator requests.

## Dependents and Personal Information
Employees adding a dependent must provide accurate information through the designated portal and respond to verification requests by the stated deadline. A dependent's sensitive data should not be placed in a public ticket, team chat, or ordinary shared document. If a name or date is entered incorrectly, the employee should contact the benefits team for a correction rather than creating a duplicate dependent record.

Only staff with an authorized business need should access benefits and dependent records. A manager should not ask an employee to disclose medical conditions or claim details to justify a routine enrollment question. If sensitive information is sent to the wrong recipient, the sender should promptly report the event through the security incident channel and follow instructions to limit further exposure.

## Coverage Changes and Payroll
The sample rule for eligible full-time employees is that coverage begins on the first day of the month following hire, but the official plan document and confirmed employee record control. An employee who changes employment type, location, hours, or leave status should ask the benefits administrator whether coverage or contributions change. Managers must not infer eligibility from a job title alone.

When employment ends, the benefits team provides applicable information about the end of active coverage and any available continuation options. Timing and rights depend on the plan and governing requirements. The assistant must not calculate a coverage end date from incomplete data. Employees should use the written notice from the administrator and raise a discrepancy promptly.

Payroll deductions depend on the selected plan, coverage tier, pay schedule, and effective date. A deduction mismatch should be reviewed by Payroll or Benefits using the employee's current election confirmation. Managers should not advise employees to stop a deduction or make a manual adjustment. If a claim is denied, the plan's explanation and appeal process control; the employer's general policy summary cannot replace the administrator's decision.

## Leave and Support
Benefits questions that arise during leave should be referred to People Operations or the plan administrator. Leave approval, payroll status, and benefit continuation are related but distinct decisions. An employee should request an explanation of any required contribution or action during leave rather than assume coverage remains unchanged. Accommodation information should be handled through the confidential process and shared only with designated reviewers.

Employees may request help comparing plan materials or understanding where to find an election. A support representative can explain how to navigate the portal but must avoid selecting a plan for the employee or recommending treatment. If a question requires interpretation of plan language, refer it to the administrator and preserve the exact question so the response addresses the relevant plan year.

## Escalations and Corrections
Contact the benefits team for eligibility, enrollment, dependent verification, or effective-date questions. Contact Payroll for a deduction issue and the plan administrator for claim or provider questions. Contact Information Security if benefits data may have been exposed. An employee who believes an enrollment record is wrong should provide the transaction date, confirmation, and a concise description through the secure channel.

People Operations should review conflicting records and document corrections in the approved system. The audit record should show who made the change, when it occurred, and the supporting confirmation, without copying unnecessary sensitive detail. If a deadline may have passed because of a system issue or unusual circumstance, route the case for formal review; do not promise an exception. The active plan documents and official annual enrollment notice take precedence over this synthetic summary.
