# Expense Reimbursement Policy

## Work-Related Expenses
The company reimburses reasonable and necessary business expenses that are pre-approved, documented, and aligned to the role's responsibilities. Typical eligible costs include approved travel, conference registration, and required equipment.

## Ineligible Expenses
Personal travel, entertainment, meals not tied to a business purpose, and duplicate purchases are not reimbursable. Expenses that are not pre-approved may be denied unless there is an emergency or required business exception.

## Equipment and Office Setup
Employees may request reimbursement for approved home office equipment if the required purchase aligns with the role and has manager approval. Business justification and an itemized receipt are required.

## Approval Before Purchase
Employees should obtain approval before committing company funds. The request should include the business purpose, estimated amount, date, cost center, vendor, attendees when relevant, and any required project or customer reference. A purchase order or procurement workflow may be required for equipment, subscriptions, or recurring services. Splitting a purchase into smaller transactions to avoid a review threshold is prohibited.

Approval must come from the manager or budget owner authorized for the expense type and amount. A verbal discussion may help with planning but does not replace the recorded approval required by the expense system. If an emergency prevents advance approval, the employee should document the circumstances and notify the manager as soon as practical. An emergency exception is reviewed after the fact and is not guaranteed to be reimbursed.

## Travel Planning
Business travel should have a defined business purpose and be approved before booking. Employees should select reasonable options that balance cost, safety, accessibility, and business needs. The traveler should use preferred vendors and booking channels when available, confirm dates and traveler names, and avoid unnecessary upgrades. Personal travel added to a business trip must be separated in the itinerary and cost calculation; the company pays only the portion attributable to approved business travel.

Changes and cancellations should be handled promptly to reduce avoidable cost. If a traveler must change plans due to safety, health, weather, or business need, they should notify the manager and preserve receipts and booking confirmations. The traveler should not book travel to a location that requires separate remote-work, security, tax, or immigration review until those approvals are complete.

Employees should use reasonable lodging near the business activity and choose a safe, appropriately priced option. Room service, entertainment, personal services, minibar purchases, and costs for companions are not business expenses unless an explicit written exception applies. Accessible accommodation may be arranged through the appropriate confidential process; employees should not be pressured to disclose private health information to justify it.

## Meals, Meetings, and Representation
Meals are reimbursable only when there is a documented business purpose and the expense is reasonable under current limits. The submission should identify attendees and their business relationship where required. Alcohol, personal meals unrelated to travel, entertainment, and meals already provided by an event or hotel are not reimbursable under this sample policy unless an authorized exception is documented. Employees should not claim per-person costs for people who did not attend.

For a group meal, the employee who pays should submit one itemized receipt and identify the business purpose, date, attendees, and allocation. Tips should be customary and within the applicable limit. A receipt showing only a card total may be insufficient if the policy requires item details. If a receipt is lost, use the expense system's missing-receipt process and provide an accurate explanation rather than recreating or altering a receipt.

Customer entertainment or gifts require additional review and may be prohibited by contract, law, or customer rules. Employees should ask Compliance before offering or accepting anything that could appear to influence a business decision. Cash equivalents and personal gifts should not be charged as ordinary business expenses.

## Expense Report Submission
Employees should submit expenses promptly within the published period, using the correct category and cost center. Each line should include an itemized receipt, business purpose, transaction date, amount, currency, and any required approval reference. Employees should verify arithmetic, remove duplicate uploads, and confirm that foreign currency conversion is shown accurately.

The submitter certifies that expenses are genuine, business-related, and not reimbursed by another party. Do not submit an expense paid directly by the company or claim the same receipt on multiple reports. If a vendor issues a refund or credit after reimbursement, notify Finance so the record can be corrected. A personal card statement alone does not demonstrate what was purchased when a detailed receipt is required.

## Review and Payment
Managers review business purpose, approval, budget, and policy compliance. Finance may request additional information, return incomplete reports, reject ineligible items, or refer a suspicious pattern for review. An approval in the workflow does not waive a policy requirement that was not visible to the approver. Employees should respond to clarification through the expense system and should not alter original receipts.

Approved reimbursement is processed according to the normal payroll or accounts-payable schedule. A pending status does not mean the expense has been accepted. Employees should contact Finance if an approved report is not paid within the expected cycle or if a payment amount differs from the approved report. Managers must not promise a payment date they do not control.

## Prohibited and Restricted Expenses
Personal travel, entertainment without an approved business purpose, duplicate purchases, traffic fines, personal subscriptions, household expenses, and costs for family members are not reimbursable. A purchase that is lawful may still be outside company policy. Items that may create a conflict of interest, violate a customer agreement, or involve a restricted party require Compliance review before commitment.

Company funds must never be used for personal cash advances, political contributions, charitable donations, or gifts in exchange for favorable treatment unless a separate authorized program expressly permits the activity. Employees must not use another employee's account or card to bypass a limit. Suspected misuse should be raised confidentially with Finance, Compliance, or People Operations.

## Equipment and Home Office Requests
Home office equipment requests should state the business need, item, expected price, and whether the request is for standard equipment, ergonomic support, or an accommodation. The manager approves business need and budget; Procurement and IT review vendor and security requirements. Employees should wait for written approval before buying an item. The equipment policy governs inventory, secure setup, return, and device configuration.

An itemized receipt and proof of payment are required for reimbursement unless Finance approves another form. An employee should not assume every chair, desk, accessory, internet plan, or utility cost is eligible. Accessibility requests should be routed through the confidential accommodation process, not evaluated solely as an ordinary discretionary purchase.

## Records, Audit, and Exceptions
Expense records must be accurate and retained in the approved system. Employees should cooperate with an audit and provide original supporting details where requested. Altered receipts, fabricated attendees, personal costs represented as business expenses, or pressure on a reviewer to ignore a discrepancy may result in corrective action.

An exception request should explain the business reason, amount, policy provision, alternatives considered, and approving owner. Approval must be documented before the transaction whenever practicable. Exceptions do not set a precedent for future expenses. Questions about tax treatment, payroll deductions, or a reimbursement dispute should be referred to Finance or Payroll. The active expense system guidance and applicable local rules control over this synthetic summary.
