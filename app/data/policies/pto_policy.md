# Paid Time Off Policy

## Purpose and Scope
This policy explains how employees earn, request, and use paid time off (PTO). It applies to full-time and part-time employees. Contractors and interns do not accrue PTO under this policy; their time off is governed by their agreement.

## Annual PTO Entitlement
Full-time employees receive 15 days of annual PTO in their first two years of service. Entitlement increases to 20 days per year after two full years of service and to 25 days per year after five full years of service. Part-time employees accrue PTO on a prorated basis according to their scheduled hours. PTO accrues each pay period, and up to 5 unused days may be carried over into the next calendar year; carried-over days expire on March 31. New employees may use accrued PTO after completing 90 days of employment, except for emergency absences approved by People Operations.

## Notice and Approval Requirements
Manager approval is required before planned PTO is taken. Planned PTO requires at least 5 business days' notice. Requests for more than 3 consecutive business days require at least 10 business days' notice and a coverage plan. An employee may not request more PTO than their available balance; requests that exceed the balance must be discussed with People Operations, who may approve unpaid time off in limited cases. Emergency absences may be approved after the fact under the emergency process below.
## Planning and Submitting a Request
Employees should submit planned PTO through the approved timekeeping or HR system as soon as dates are known. The request should include dates, whether the employee expects to be unavailable for full or partial days, and a basic coverage plan for active responsibilities. Employees should not include medical details or sensitive personal information in a routine vacation request. If the system is unavailable, notify the manager and People Operations through an approved channel and enter the request once service is restored.

The request remains pending until the authorized manager approves it in the system. A calendar invitation or informal chat message does not establish approval. Employees should wait for confirmation before making nonrefundable arrangements. A manager may ask for clarification about coverage or scheduling, but should not request unnecessary personal details. The employee should receive a response in a reasonable period and can contact People Operations if the request remains unresolved.

Planned PTO requires at least five business days' notice, and a predictable vacation of more than three consecutive business days should be requested at least ten business days in advance. These notice periods are requirements for planned time off, but they are not a substitute for the current local policy or a guarantee of approval. Unexpected events may make advance notice impossible. The employee should inform the manager as soon as practicable and follow the emergency absence process rather than entering inaccurate dates.

## Manager Review and Coverage
Managers consider available balance, timing and duration, staffing levels, deadlines, customer commitments, and fairness across the team. Approval should be based on consistent business criteria, not retaliation or favoritism. Managers should consider requests in the order and manner required by the current process and explain a denial or proposed alternative clearly. When requested dates cannot be supported, discuss workable alternatives where practical.

For absences longer than three consecutive business days, the employee and manager should identify critical tasks, handoff contacts, deadlines, and required approvals. A coverage plan should be proportionate and must not make the employee continuously available during approved PTO. Sensitive information should be transferred only through authorized systems, and access should follow least-privilege rules. The manager is responsible for assigning a temporary owner for urgent work.

Team coverage planning does not change an employee's balance or guarantee approval. Managers should avoid creating an informal policy that blocks all leave during busy periods without review by People Operations. If multiple requests conflict, the manager should apply clear, documented criteria and consider alternatives. An employee may ask People Operations to review an unresolved procedural concern.

## Balance, Accrual, and Corrections
The HR system is the authoritative source for the current PTO balance. The employee data in this project is synthetic and must not be treated as a live balance. Accrual depends on employment status and tenure, and this policy summary does not calculate an individual employee's entitlement. Employees should review their balance before planning leave and report a suspected discrepancy through Payroll or People Operations with the relevant dates and transaction details.

Managers must not manually alter a balance outside the approved workflow. Corrections should preserve an audit trail showing the original entry, reason, approver, and effective date. If employment status changes, People Operations should explain how accrual and any unused balance are handled under applicable terms. This document does not promise payout or carryover.

## Changes, Cancellation, and Holidays
If dates change, the employee should update or cancel the request in the HR system and notify the manager. The manager should confirm whether revised dates can be supported. Employees should not simply remain absent on dates that were not approved. If a manager changes an approved request because of an unexpected business need, People Operations should be consulted and the employee should be given a clear explanation and practical alternatives.

A company holiday during a requested period is handled according to the current holiday calendar and timekeeping rules. Employees should not assume whether a holiday consumes PTO; confirm the correct code in the system. The holiday policy covers calendar and closure questions. A holiday does not automatically extend a separate leave period unless the applicable record says so.

## Emergency and Unplanned Absence
For illness, urgent family needs, safety events, or other unexpected circumstances, notify the manager as soon as reasonably possible. If the employee cannot contact the manager, a representative may do so where appropriate. Use the confidential People Operations channel if the situation may involve medical, bereavement, protected, or other special leave. Managers should not demand sensitive details in a public team channel.

An emergency absence can be recorded after the fact when advance notice was not possible. The employee should accurately report dates and hours and respond to reasonable follow-up through the secure process. The manager should not treat an emergency as planned vacation or promise that PTO is the only applicable option. People Operations determines whether another leave process applies and explains documentation requirements.

## Special Leave and Return
Medical, parental, bereavement, caregiver, civic, and military leave may be governed by separate rules. PTO may run concurrently only when the applicable process permits it and the employee has been informed how time will be recorded. An employee should contact People Operations when an absence may qualify for a separate leave or accommodation review. The manager should not make a legal eligibility determination from this summary.

Employees should not be asked to disclose a diagnosis to obtain routine scheduling help. If certification or supporting documentation is required, People Operations will explain what is needed and how to submit it securely. Benefit eligibility and pay status during leave are separate questions that should be confirmed with the benefits administrator or Payroll.

Before a planned return, the employee should confirm the expected date if the HR system requests it. If the absence will be longer, contact the manager and People Operations as early as possible. Managers should coordinate work reassignment and access restoration through approved systems and should not pressure an employee to perform work while on approved PTO.

Questions about request status, balance, a denial, or special leave should go to the manager or People Operations. Payroll handles balance and time-record corrections. The HR assistant can provide general policy text and mock workflow guidance but cannot approve leave or change a real balance. The official policy, employment terms, and applicable local rules control over this synthetic example.
