# Data Security and Acceptable Use Policy

## Required Controls
All employees must use approved accounts and multifactor authentication on company systems. Workstations must lock automatically after a period of inactivity, and company devices must avoid unsecured public Wi-Fi for sensitive operations.

## Confidential Data
Confidential employee data, payroll records, and identity information must be accessed only for valid business purposes. Downloading or copying sensitive data to personal devices is prohibited unless explicitly approved by Information Security.

## Remote Work Security
Remote work requires use of approved VPN access, company-managed devices, and secure collaboration tools. Any device not approved for remote work may not be used to access employee benefits or payroll systems.

## Identity and Account Protection
Each worker must use an individually assigned company account. Shared accounts are not permitted for ordinary work because they prevent accurate attribution and weaken access reviews. Multifactor authentication must be enabled wherever the service supports it. Employees should use an approved authenticator or security key and should never disclose a one-time code to a caller, message sender, coworker, or purported support agent. The IT service desk will not ask an employee to reveal a password or authentication code.

Passwords should be unique to company services and stored only in an approved password manager. Employees should not reuse a personal password for a company account. If a password is entered into a suspicious site or disclosed accidentally, the employee should change it through the official sign-in page and report the event to the service desk. A password reset does not replace reporting a possible account compromise.

Employees should verify the destination and context before approving an unexpected login prompt. Repeated authentication prompts, unfamiliar device notices, or a request to install remote-control software should be treated as suspicious. Deny prompts that the employee did not initiate and contact the service desk using a known company channel. Do not use contact information supplied only by the suspicious message.

## Data Classification and Minimization
Employees should access only the information necessary for assigned duties. Sensitive employee, payroll, identity, benefits, and customer information must be handled using approved systems with appropriate access controls. Before sharing a file, confirm the recipients, permission scope, and business purpose. Links that allow anyone with the link to view a file should not be used for confidential information.

Collect and retain only information needed for a legitimate business task. Avoid downloading a large dataset when a filtered report will meet the need. Do not include personal identifiers in a subject line or public issue tracker. When a report can be shared in aggregate, prefer that form to individual-level data. If a recipient does not need a field, remove it before sending.

Data should not be moved to personal email, consumer file-sharing accounts, unauthorized AI services, or removable media. Employees must not paste confidential records into an external chatbot or public code repository. A manager's approval alone does not authorize a transfer that Information Security has restricted. Exceptions require review by the designated data owner and security team, documented scope, an expiration date, and safeguards.

## Device and Workspace Security
Company devices must use supported software and remain enrolled in endpoint management. Employees should install updates through the approved process and restart when requested. Security controls must not be disabled to improve convenience or performance. If an update prevents essential work, contact the service desk for a supported resolution rather than bypassing the control.

When stepping away, lock the screen even in a private office. Position screens so visitors cannot read sensitive information. Printed records should be collected promptly from shared printers and stored in an approved locked location. Conversations involving confidential information should not take place where they can be overheard. Employees working in a shared home should avoid displaying records to household members or using shared accounts and devices.

Public networks present additional risks. Use approved VPN or secure access controls and avoid sensitive transactions on an untrusted connection. Do not connect unknown USB devices or charging stations that request data access. If a mobile hotspot is used, secure it with a unique password and current device software. Travel and remote-work approvals remain subject to location restrictions in the applicable policy.

## Email, Messaging, and Social Engineering
Before acting on a request to send money, change payroll details, share employee data, or reset access, verify it through a second trusted channel. Attackers may imitate executives, HR staff, vendors, or IT support and may use urgency or secrecy to pressure the recipient. Unexpected attachments and links should be reported using the mail client's reporting control. Do not forward suspicious content broadly or reply to ask whether it is legitimate.

External recipients should be checked carefully, especially when autocomplete suggests a similar name. Use approved encryption or secure sharing for confidential files. Do not send a password in the same message as a protected file. Meeting invitations and chat messages can contain malicious links; a familiar display name does not prove the sender's identity.

Employees should use approved collaboration tools and retain business records according to the retention schedule. Sensitive HR cases should use restricted workspaces, not team channels. Messages should not be deleted or edited to conceal a security event, investigation, or business decision. If information was sent to the wrong recipient, notify the security incident channel immediately and do not attempt to conceal the event.

## Access Requests and Reviews
Access must be approved by the data or system owner and limited to the role's needs. Requests should state the user, system, business purpose, access level, and expected duration. Privileged access requires stronger review and should be used only for approved administrative tasks. Temporary access should expire automatically where possible. Employees must not borrow another person's credentials to work around a missing permission.

Managers and system owners should review access when an employee changes role, location, team, or employment status. Access that is no longer needed should be removed promptly. Service accounts and integrations require an accountable owner, a documented purpose, secure credential storage, and periodic review. Credentials must not be embedded in source code, spreadsheets, or project documentation.

## Incident Reporting and Response
Report suspected phishing, lost devices, malware, unauthorized access, accidental disclosure, or an unusual account event promptly through the security incident channel or service desk. Include what happened, approximate time, systems involved, and actions already taken. Do not include passwords or unnecessary personal information in the report. If there is immediate physical danger, contact the appropriate emergency service first.

Employees should preserve relevant messages, logs, and devices and follow the response team's instructions. Do not wipe a device, delete a message, contact a suspected attacker, or run an unapproved cleanup utility unless directed. Security may disable an account, revoke a session, preserve logs, or isolate a device to contain risk. Such actions are protective and do not imply fault.

The response team evaluates scope, affected data, notification obligations, and recovery steps with the appropriate owners. Employees should direct outside inquiries to the designated communications or legal contact and should not speculate publicly. Lessons learned may result in new controls or training. Employees are expected to cooperate honestly and promptly.

## Retention, Exceptions, and Training
Business information should be retained according to the official records schedule and any legal hold or investigation instruction. Employees must not destroy relevant records when a hold is active. Devices and storage media should be returned to IT for approved sanitization or disposal. Paper containing confidential information should be placed in an approved secure destruction container, not ordinary recycling.

Third-party access requires a documented business need, contract review, security assessment, least-privilege permissions, and an accountable internal owner. Employees should not upload company data to a vendor trial or free service without approval. Data sharing should specify purpose, scope, retention, deletion, incident notification, and review dates.

Employees must complete assigned security awareness training and any role-specific training by the published deadline. Exceptions require a written request, business purpose, affected data and systems, compensating controls, owner, and expiration date. The security team may reject or revoke an exception when risk changes. The current security standard and incident response procedure control when more specific guidance exists.
