# Remote Work and Travel Policy

## Purpose
This policy describes when employees may work remotely, travel for work, and operate outside their assigned office location. The policy supports business continuity while maintaining security and compliance requirements.

## Eligibility
Employees may work remotely up to 2 days per week if they are in a jurisdiction approved by People Ops and legal. Any work outside the employee's home state or country for longer than 2 weeks requires manager approval and a compliance review.

## Security and Compliance
All remote workers must complete annual security training. Employees must use approved company devices and secure VPN access. Access to confidential payroll, customer support, and benefits systems is restricted to approved users only.

## Documentation
Remote work requests should include the employee's location, travel dates, manager approval, and business justification. Requests for international work beyond 30 days require additional tax and immigration review.

## Decision Rule
If the employee's proposed location is not listed in the approved remote work map or if the request is longer than 30 days outside the home market, a manager and HR review is required before approval.

## Request and Approval Workflow
Employees should submit a remote-work request before making travel commitments or beginning work from a new jurisdiction. The request should state the work location, home location, start and end dates, time zone, business reason, expected work schedule, and any customer or data access needs. The manager reviews role suitability and team coverage. People Operations and designated compliance reviewers determine location eligibility, employment implications, and any additional approval needed.

Approval is specific to the stated employee, location, dates, and work arrangement. A prior approval for a different trip or another employee does not establish approval for the current request. A request remains pending until all required reviewers record a decision. Employees should not treat silence or a travel booking as approval. If the location or dates change, the employee must notify the manager and request an updated review before working under the changed conditions.

The review may consider local employment rules, tax presence, immigration status, insurance coverage, data access, customer requirements, security controls, time-zone overlap, and the employee's role. Not all reviewers are needed for every request. The responsible teams decide which checks apply and may limit or decline a request if requirements cannot be met. The assistant can describe policy factors but cannot provide tax or immigration advice or approve the location.

## Short-Term Domestic Work
Working outside the employee's regular home state for more than two weeks requires manager approval and a compliance review under this sample policy. Shorter periods may still require approval if the jurisdiction is not on the approved list, the employee handles restricted information, or local rules require review. Employees should confirm both the number of workdays and calendar dates because travel dates and work dates may differ.

Before departure, the employee should confirm the location is approved, the work schedule is agreed, the manager has a coverage plan, and secure access is available. A short stay with family or a temporary change in lodging does not automatically exempt the employee from location rules. Employees may not perform work from a restricted destination simply because they are on vacation or visiting briefly.

Managers should consider customer meetings, team handoffs, required in-person responsibilities, and practical working hours. If time-zone differences affect service, the employee and manager should agree on availability and response expectations in writing. Approved remote work does not require the employee to be available outside their normal schedule or during approved PTO.

## International Work and Travel
International work involves additional review and must be approved before work begins. Requests exceeding 30 days outside the home market require tax and immigration review in this sample policy. A shorter stay may still require review due to destination restrictions, customer commitments, data handling, or employment status. A tourist entry permission should not be assumed to authorize work. The employee must obtain advice from the appropriate compliance team and must not rely on a general chat response to decide immigration status.

The request should identify each country, work dates, personal travel dates, and systems or data that will be accessed. The employee should confirm that company equipment can be used lawfully and securely at the destination. International travel may restrict access to certain systems or data. The security team may require additional controls, prohibit local storage, or provide a managed device. An employee must not route traffic through a personal VPN or another person's account to bypass a restriction.

If a trip combines business and personal days, separate the schedules and expense treatment. The approved business period does not automatically authorize work during personal travel days or after the approved end date. Request a revised decision if an airline disruption, family emergency, or business event changes the stay. A manager cannot waive a legal or security restriction.

## Security and Equipment Requirements
Employees working remotely must use a company-managed and approved device, secure VPN or other approved access method, multifactor authentication, and authorized collaboration tools. Annual security training must be complete before remote access is granted. The employee should lock the device when unattended, protect the screen from observation, and avoid discussing confidential information in shared or public spaces.

Confidential payroll, benefits, employee, and customer information must be accessed only for a valid business purpose. Personal devices may not be used to download or store this information unless Information Security has approved a documented exception. Public Wi-Fi should not be used for sensitive work unless the approved secure connection is active and the environment is appropriate. Lost devices, suspicious authentication prompts, and suspected disclosures must be reported promptly.

The manager should confirm role access is still necessary and request changes through the normal access process. Remote work does not permit exporting a full dataset for convenience or copying records into personal cloud storage. Employees should use the least amount of information needed and approved systems for file transfer. The data security policy governs handling details if it is more specific.

## Work Environment and Changes
Employees are responsible for selecting a workspace that supports safe, focused work and protects company information. The company may offer equipment or an accommodation process, but employees should not make an unapproved purchase and assume reimbursement. Requests for ergonomic or disability-related support should be routed confidentially through People Operations.

Remote work does not change a manager's responsibility to provide clear expectations, feedback, and team support. Managers should establish appropriate check-ins without requiring continuous monitoring. Employees should report a workplace injury or safety concern through the appropriate process. The assistant cannot assess an emergency; employees should contact local emergency services when immediate assistance is needed.

Employees should promptly report a change to work location, travel dates, manager, role, or employment status. Managers should review recurring arrangements periodically and end permissions when the approved period expires. A change in job duties may require a new assessment if system access or customer obligations change. Work records remain subject to the company's retention and legal-hold requirements regardless of where an employee is working.

## Denial, Exceptions, and Questions
If a request is declined, the responsible reviewer should explain the relevant business or compliance constraint to the extent it can be shared and discuss whether another location or schedule is possible. An employee may request clarification or submit materially new information. An exception must be documented, time-limited, and approved by every required owner; an informal manager message does not override a security or legal restriction.

Questions about employee eligibility should go to People Operations, security controls to Information Security, and tax or immigration issues to the designated qualified reviewer. The current approved location list and official compliance instructions control over this synthetic policy summary. The assistant's response is informational and does not constitute approval to travel or work from a different jurisdiction.
