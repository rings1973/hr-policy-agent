# Company Holiday and Scheduling Policy

Synthetic policy example for this educational application. It does not describe a real employer's commitments and must not be treated as legal advice.

## Purpose and Scope
This policy explains how the sample company publishes holidays, plans coverage, records holiday time, and responds to closures. It applies to employees unless a written agreement, applicable local rule, or People Operations notice provides a different rule. Contractors should follow the holiday terms in their service agreement and should ask their contact before assuming an employee holiday schedule applies. The official annual calendar is maintained by People Operations; a prior-year calendar is not authoritative for the current year.

## Annual Calendar
People Operations publishes the next calendar year's planned holidays before the beginning of that year. The calendar identifies the date observed, the holiday name, and whether a closure applies company-wide or only to specified offices. A calendar may list a weekday in place of a holiday that falls on a weekend. The observed date, not the historic date of the holiday, is used to plan staffing unless the calendar says otherwise. Managers should communicate any team-specific coverage rotation before the first affected holiday.

The sample calendar normally includes New Year's Day, a spring observance, Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, a late-November holiday, a winter holiday, and one flexible personal observance day. These examples are not a promise of a particular paid schedule. The current company calendar and applicable employment terms control. Local offices may have additional holidays or a different observed date. People Operations should review proposed calendar changes with affected teams and give reasonable notice when the business requires a change.

## Eligibility and Pay Questions
The applicable offer, employment agreement, location-specific addendum, and current People Operations notice determine an employee's holiday pay eligibility. Full-time employees in this synthetic example are generally scheduled off on company-wide holidays. Part-time schedules, temporary assignments, and non-employee engagements may differ. This policy does not establish overtime, premium pay, or wage entitlements; payroll questions should be referred to Payroll or People Operations for the employee's work location.

An employee who is required to work on a scheduled holiday should receive advance notice where practicable. The manager records the business reason and confirms the correct timekeeping code with Payroll. Employees must record actual hours accurately and must not enter holiday hours for time they did not work. Managers may not ask employees to edit time records to conceal overtime or hours worked. If a time entry is incorrect, the employee should promptly submit a correction using the approved timekeeping process.

## Team Coverage and Customer Commitments
A manager may arrange a limited coverage rotation for support, security, facilities, or other essential work. Coverage should be shared fairly and communicated in advance, including the expected hours, handoff contact, and escalation channel. A coverage plan must not assume that an employee is continuously available while recorded as off duty. Teams should update customer and partner contacts when normal response times will change.

Employees who are not assigned holiday coverage should not be expected to monitor company systems during time off. If a genuine emergency requires contact, the manager should use the published escalation channel, explain the request, and record time worked in accordance with applicable process. A manager should not create an informal on-call expectation through repeated messages or by treating a holiday as an ordinary workday.

## Flexible Observance and Religious Accommodation
Employees may ask to use the flexible personal observance day or request a schedule adjustment for a religious, cultural, civic, or personal observance. Requests should be made to the manager with reasonable notice when the date is known. The employee is not required to disclose sensitive beliefs to teammates. Managers should discuss workable scheduling options and involve People Operations if an accommodation request is unclear or cannot be resolved directly.

A request should not be denied merely because the observance is unfamiliar to the manager. Coverage needs may affect timing, but decisions should be consistent, respectful, and based on operational requirements. The manager should avoid asking for unnecessary personal details. People Operations can help review competing coverage requests or accommodations without disclosing confidential details to the team.

## Holiday and PTO Coordination
A company holiday that falls during a scheduled PTO period is handled according to the current calendar and timekeeping instructions. Employees should ask Payroll or People Operations how to record the specific situation rather than assuming the day consumes a PTO balance. A holiday does not automatically extend an approved leave period unless the applicable leave record or policy says so. Employees should submit ordinary planned PTO requests through the approved process and obtain manager approval before making nonrefundable plans.

When a holiday is adjacent to a planned absence, managers may request a coverage plan if the absence affects a critical service. Coverage planning does not change an employee's available balance or guarantee approval. Employees should not enter a holiday as PTO simply to avoid using the proper calendar code.

## Office Closure and Severe Conditions
The company may close an office or change to remote operations because of severe weather, building issues, public safety events, or utility outages. People Operations or the designated incident lead communicates the decision through approved channels. A local office closure does not necessarily mean that every employee in every region is off work. Employees should follow the message for their assigned location and ask their manager when the instruction is unclear.

During a closure, employees should prioritize personal safety and should not travel to a closed facility without authorization. Employees who cannot work because of an outage should notify their manager and follow the current timekeeping guidance. Managers should not direct employees to use an unsafe connection or personal device to continue sensitive work. Security and incident reporting requirements remain in effect during remote operations.

## Questions and Corrections
Employees should direct calendar, eligibility, pay-code, or schedule questions to the manager, People Operations, or Payroll as appropriate. A disagreement about hours worked should be raised promptly through Payroll's correction channel. People Operations reviews calendar exceptions and may update this policy or the annual calendar. The version and effective date published by People Operations control if this sample text conflicts with a newer official notice.
