# Workplace Conduct and Respectful Workplace Policy

Synthetic policy example for an educational assistant. It is a general workplace procedure, not legal advice, a finding about any person, or a substitute for a current official policy.

## Purpose and Scope
The company expects a respectful, professional, and safe workplace. This policy describes expected conduct, reporting options, and the general process for reviewing concerns. It applies to employees and, where relevant, contractors, visitors, vendors, and people participating in company work events. A local policy or applicable law may provide additional rights or procedures. People Operations is responsible for interpreting this sample policy and coordinating sensitive cases.

The policy does not require employees to tolerate threatening, harassing, discriminatory, or retaliatory conduct. It also does not predetermine that an allegation is true. Concerns are reviewed fairly, information is limited to people who need it, and the company may take appropriate interim steps while gathering facts.

## Expected Conduct
Employees should communicate honestly, treat coworkers and customers with dignity, respect professional boundaries, and follow reasonable safety instructions. Disagreement about work should focus on evidence, responsibilities, and outcomes rather than personal attacks. Employees should not use slurs, unwanted sexual comments, intimidation, threats, humiliation, or repeated conduct intended to isolate or undermine another person.

The same expectations apply in meetings, messaging tools, email, company-sponsored events, business travel, and remote work. Text-based communication can be misunderstood, so employees should avoid hostile or demeaning language and use appropriate channels for difficult conversations. A manager should model respectful feedback, explain work decisions clearly, and address concerns without public shaming.

Employees must follow access, privacy, security, safety, and records-management rules. They should use confidential employee information only for legitimate business purposes and should not discuss sensitive HR matters in public spaces or informal channels. Personal conflicts of interest, gifts, or outside activities that may affect impartial work should be disclosed through the appropriate manager or compliance process.

## Prohibited Behavior
Prohibited behavior includes harassment, discrimination, bullying, threats, violence, stalking, retaliation, deliberate misuse of confidential information, falsification of business records, and conduct that creates an unsafe work environment. A single serious incident may warrant review; a pattern of less severe actions may also require attention. This policy does not attempt to define legal standards or decide whether a specific event violates law.

Employees should not retaliate against someone for making a good-faith report, assisting an inquiry, requesting an accommodation, or participating in a workplace review. Retaliation can include threats, exclusion from work opportunities, unjustified negative treatment, or pressure to withdraw a report. Ordinary performance feedback or a documented business decision is not automatically retaliation, but it should be handled consistently and without punishing protected reporting.

## Reporting a Concern
An employee may report a concern to their manager, another manager, People Operations, the designated ethics channel, or another contact identified in the current company directory. If the concern involves the employee's manager, the employee may bypass that manager. If there is immediate danger, the employee should contact emergency services or local security first and then notify the appropriate company contact when safe.

A report may include what happened, approximate dates, location or communication channel, people involved, witnesses, and any immediate safety concern. Employees should share facts they know and distinguish direct observation from information they heard from someone else. They should not conduct their own investigation, access another person's accounts, circulate allegations broadly, or record conversations unlawfully. A report can be made even if the employee does not have every detail or is unsure which policy applies.

People Operations may ask follow-up questions to understand the concern and identify records or witnesses. The employee should preserve relevant messages or documents and avoid altering or deleting potentially relevant material. The company does not promise absolute confidentiality; information may need to be shared with investigators, decision-makers, or others who must respond. Access is limited to the extent practical and appropriate.

## Review and Investigation Process
People Operations or a designated reviewer determines the appropriate response. The process may include intake, a safety assessment, interim measures, interviews, review of relevant records, credibility assessment, and a decision about next steps. The reviewer may speak with the reporting person, the person whose conduct is questioned, and witnesses. Each person should have an opportunity to provide relevant information.

The company may separate work assignments, adjust access, or use another temporary measure while a review is pending. Interim measures are not a finding of wrongdoing and should be designed to reduce risk and disruption. Employees must cooperate honestly and should not pressure witnesses, coordinate stories, or interfere with records. If new concerns arise, they should be reported through an available channel.

The length and scope of a review depend on the facts, availability of information, and applicable process. People Operations may not be able to share every detail or another employee's personnel outcome. The reporting person should receive an appropriate status update when possible, but no specific timetable or result is guaranteed by this sample policy.

## Outcomes and Corrective Action
If the review identifies a policy concern, the company may take corrective action consistent with the facts, the seriousness and repetition of conduct, prior relevant history, and applicable requirements. Actions may include coaching, training, changes to work arrangements, access restrictions, formal discipline, or separation from the company. The company may also take steps when evidence is inconclusive but a practical safety or workplace adjustment is appropriate.

A report made in good faith is not misconduct merely because the review cannot substantiate it. Knowingly making a false report, destroying evidence, or interfering with a review may itself result in corrective action. Managers should not promise a particular outcome or disclose confidential personnel information to coworkers.

## Manager Responsibilities
Managers who receive a concern should listen without judging, record only necessary factual information, avoid promising secrecy, and promptly contact People Operations. They should not confront the person accused, mediate a serious safety concern informally, or discourage a report. A manager should take immediate practical steps if there is an urgent threat, following safety procedures and contacting the appropriate responders.

Managers must preserve relevant work records and avoid making changes that could appear punitive because a concern was raised. They should consult People Operations before changing assignments, access, schedules, or performance expectations for a person involved in a pending review. A manager who is personally involved in the matter should disclose that fact and step back from decisions where appropriate.

## Support, Accommodation, and External Resources
Employees may ask People Operations about available support, accommodation, leave, or an employee assistance resource. A workplace conduct report and an accommodation request are related but separate processes; one does not automatically resolve the other. Employees should use the confidential channel for medical or personal information and share only what is needed for the relevant review.

The company cannot prevent an employee from contacting an appropriate government agency, law enforcement, or outside professional. The assistant is not a substitute for emergency support, legal counsel, or a formal reporting channel. Questions about legal rights or external deadlines should be directed to a qualified professional or relevant agency.

## Recordkeeping and Review
People Operations stores case information in approved restricted-access systems and retains it under the applicable schedule. Managers should not keep parallel files in team drives or personal notes beyond necessary business records. People Operations periodically reviews training and reporting processes and may revise this policy. If this synthetic example differs from a current official procedure, the official procedure and applicable law control.
