# Company Equipment and Home Office Policy

Synthetic policy example for an educational HR assistant. All named procedures and limits are illustrative; employees must consult the current official process for a real decision.

## Purpose and Scope
This policy covers company-issued devices, accessories, software, and the setup of a home work area. Its goals are to protect company information, keep equipment usable, provide a clear request path, and avoid unapproved purchases. It supplements the data security, remote work, and expense policies. It does not grant an employee ownership of company property or promise reimbursement for an item purchased without required approval.

## Issuance and Inventory
A manager requests equipment through the approved service desk or procurement workflow. The request should identify the employee, role, primary work location, business need, and requested delivery date. Standard equipment may include a managed laptop, power supply, headset, and role-specific accessories. Additional monitors, docking stations, ergonomic items, or specialized hardware require a business justification and any required budget approval.

The service desk records the item, serial number where applicable, assignment date, and return status in the approved inventory system. Employees should inspect delivered equipment and report damage, missing items, or incorrect shipments promptly. Equipment is provided for business use and remains company property. Employees must not remove inventory labels, lend a device to another person, or allow family members or other unauthorized users to sign in.

## Secure Configuration and Use
Company-managed devices must use the approved operating system configuration, endpoint protection, screen lock, multifactor authentication, and security updates. Employees should connect using approved secure access methods and use only authorized collaboration and file-storage services. Local administrator rights, unapproved browser extensions, personal cloud storage, and unauthorized software are not permitted unless Information Security has approved an exception.

Employees must not disable endpoint protection, bypass device management, share passwords, or store confidential payroll, benefits, customer, or employee records on an unmanaged personal device. Removable storage should not be used for company information unless specifically authorized and encrypted under the security team's requirements. A personal printer or home network should not be used to create unnecessary copies of sensitive records. Paper records containing confidential information must be protected from household visitors and disposed of through an approved method.

When traveling or working away from the assigned office, employees should keep devices physically secure, avoid leaving equipment visible in a vehicle, and use a private workspace for sensitive conversations. Public Wi-Fi should not be used for sensitive operations unless the approved secure connection is active and the employee can protect the screen from observation. Devices should not be checked as luggage when a safer method of transport is available. Remote-work location approvals and international restrictions remain governed by the remote-work policy.

## Purchase Requests and Reimbursement
Employees should request standard equipment through the manager and procurement process before buying it. A purchase is not reimbursable merely because it is useful or appears on a list of examples. The request should include the business purpose, item description, expected cost, vendor, and any accessibility or role requirement. The manager confirms the budget and required approval before an order is placed. Procurement may select an equivalent item or supplier to meet security, warranty, or cost requirements.

A home-office expense may qualify only when the expense policy permits it and the required manager approval is recorded in advance. The employee should submit an itemized receipt and business justification through the approved expense system within the published submission period. Personal furniture upgrades, duplicate items, entertainment devices, and purchases for another household member are not eligible under this sample policy unless a written exception is approved. Employees should not split transactions to avoid an approval threshold.

Reimbursement is not guaranteed until the authorized reviewer approves the complete submission. If an item is denied, the employee should not resubmit the same cost under a different category without new information. Questions about tax treatment, payroll deductions, or ownership of reimbursed equipment should go to Payroll or People Operations.

## Ergonomics and Accessibility
Employees may request an ergonomic assessment or accessibility-related equipment through the confidential accommodation process. The employee should share only information needed by the designated team to evaluate the request; managers and coworkers should not request detailed medical records. People Operations may coordinate with Facilities, Information Technology, or an accommodation specialist. A standard procurement path should not be used to bypass a confidential accommodation review.

Employees should report equipment that prevents them from performing essential work safely. A temporary workaround may be considered while a request is reviewed, but it must continue to protect company information. Managers should not promise an accommodation decision before the responsible team completes its review.

## Loss, Damage, and Security Events
A lost or stolen company device, suspected malware, unusual sign-in prompt, or accidental exposure of data must be reported promptly through the service desk and security incident channel. Employees should not attempt to conceal the incident or perform an unauthorized forensic investigation. The security team may remotely lock or wipe a company-managed device to protect data. Employees should provide accurate facts, including approximate time and location, and preserve related messages when asked.

Normal wear should be reported so that repair or replacement can be arranged. Employees must not use personal repair services, remove storage components, or dispose of a device without written direction. The service desk determines whether a device should be repaired, replaced, or returned. Managers should not deduct equipment cost from wages or make an employee personally responsible without review by People Operations and Payroll under applicable requirements.

## Role Changes, Leave, and Return
When an employee changes roles, transfers locations, begins an extended leave, or separates from employment, the manager and service desk review assigned equipment and access. The employee receives instructions for secure return, shipping materials if needed, and a deadline appropriate to the situation. Employees must not retain company data after access ends or copy files for personal use. If a return is delayed by disability, leave, travel, or another practical barrier, the employee should contact People Operations for an alternative arrangement.

Returned devices are checked into inventory, wiped or reconfigured by authorized staff, and reassigned only after required security steps. Managers should not accept a device informally without notifying the service desk, because inventory and access records must remain accurate.

## Exceptions and Review
An exception request should identify the equipment, business need, duration, data involved, and proposed safeguards. Information Security must review exceptions that affect device management, storage, network access, or confidential information. Procurement and the manager review cost and vendor requirements. People Operations reviews accessibility, employee-relations, and separation concerns. Approval from one reviewer does not substitute for another required approval. The policy owner reviews this procedure periodically and may publish more specific standards for particular roles or locations.
