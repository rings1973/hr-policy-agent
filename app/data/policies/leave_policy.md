# Leave and Accommodation Policy

Synthetic policy example for an educational assistant. It is not legal advice, does not determine eligibility under any real law, and does not replace the current official policy or a written employment agreement.

## Purpose and Scope
This policy explains how employees can request time away from work for health, family, bereavement, civic service, military service, or other significant needs. The separate PTO policy covers ordinary planned vacation and short personal time. Leave may be paid, unpaid, protected, or coordinated with other benefits depending on the employee's location, employment terms, and circumstances. People Operations determines which process applies and explains applicable notices, documentation, pay coordination, and return-to-work steps.

The examples below describe routing and communication, not a guarantee that a particular leave type is available. Employment type, tenure, work location, plan terms, and applicable law can change the outcome. Managers should promptly refer a possible leave or accommodation request to People Operations rather than making a final eligibility decision themselves.

## How to Request Leave
When practicable, an employee should contact People Operations and their manager as soon as they know leave may be needed. The employee should provide the anticipated start date, expected duration if known, and a reliable contact method. For an emergency or unexpected absence, the employee or a representative may notify the manager as soon as reasonably possible; advance notice is not always possible. An employee should not delay contacting People Operations merely because a medical provider's paperwork is not yet available.

The employee should use the confidential HR channel for sensitive health, family, or personal information. A manager generally needs to know the expected work absence and practical coverage information, not a diagnosis or detailed personal history. People Operations may request documentation where permitted and necessary to administer the leave or accommodation. Documents should be sent only through an approved secure process, not to a team chat or shared folder.

People Operations confirms receipt, identifies any missing information, explains the applicable review path, and provides a written status when practicable. A request is not approved solely because a form was submitted. The employee should follow the existing attendance process while a decision is pending unless People Operations provides another instruction. Managers should not promise a decision date they cannot control.

## Medical Leave and Workplace Adjustments
An employee who needs time away or a work adjustment for a health condition should contact People Operations. The review may consider leave, a modified schedule, changes to work location, equipment, duties, or another reasonable adjustment. The employee is not required to share medical details with coworkers. People Operations may coordinate with an accommodation specialist or a benefits administrator and will limit information shared with managers to what is needed to plan work and implement an approved adjustment.

A manager should not independently reject a request, ask intrusive questions, or pressure an employee to work while on an approved leave. If the employee can perform some tasks or work a modified schedule, the responsible HR reviewer should assess the request and any operational limits. An adjustment should be documented with its effective dates and any agreed review date. Changes in the employee's needs should be reported through the confidential channel so the arrangement can be revisited.

## Parental, Caregiver, and Family Leave
Employees planning a birth, adoption, foster placement, or significant caregiving responsibility should contact People Operations as early as practicable. The reviewer explains any company leave, benefits, scheduling, and return-to-work steps that may apply. The timing of leave may involve more than one process, and an employee should not assume that a PTO balance alone answers eligibility or pay questions.

Managers should plan coverage based on expected work dates and transition needs. They should not ask an employee to disclose private family details to the team. If a return date is uncertain, People Operations can explain how updates should be provided and how a temporary coverage plan may be adjusted. Any benefit continuation or enrollment deadlines should be confirmed with the appropriate benefits administrator.

## Bereavement and Urgent Family Needs
An employee experiencing a death or urgent family event should notify the manager when reasonably possible and contact People Operations to determine the applicable leave or PTO process. Managers should respond with compassion and avoid requiring public disclosure of the circumstances. The appropriate duration, paid status, and documentation depend on the current company policy and applicable employment terms. If additional time is needed, the employee should contact People Operations before the currently approved period ends where possible.

For an immediate safety or health emergency, employees should contact emergency services or the relevant local resource first. The HR assistant cannot assess emergencies. People Operations can help identify a suitable workplace process after the immediate situation is safe.

## Civic, Jury, and Military Service
Employees who receive a jury summons, subpoena, military duty notice, or other official service request should contact People Operations and their manager promptly. They should provide only the notice or scheduling details requested through the approved channel. People Operations reviews applicable protections, pay treatment, timekeeping, and required records. Managers should not discourage lawful service or make a final decision about the employee's rights based on an informal summary.

If service dates change, the employee should update People Operations and the manager as soon as practicable. The company may request proof of dates served when permitted and relevant to timekeeping. Sensitive information should be stored in the designated HR record and not in shared team notes.

## PTO Coordination and Other Benefits
PTO may be available for some absences, but its use does not replace a separate leave review when one is required. People Operations explains whether accrued time is applied, whether an employee may choose to use PTO, and how payroll records the absence. Employees should not assume that paid time changes the length or status of a leave. Benefit eligibility and continuation are governed by the applicable plan materials, not by the PTO balance.

Employees should avoid submitting inconsistent absence records in multiple systems. If an employee has already submitted PTO for dates that later become part of a leave request, they should tell People Operations so the records can be reconciled. A manager should not edit an employee's balance or time record outside the approved process.

## Communication, Coverage, and Confidentiality
Before leave begins, the employee and manager may agree on a reasonable handoff of active work, key deadlines, and emergency contacts. The handoff should not require the employee to remain available during approved leave. A manager should appoint a temporary point of contact and restrict access to work materials according to business need and security policy.

The employee should follow the check-in schedule provided by People Operations, especially when dates change or additional information is needed. The manager should not contact an employee repeatedly for routine tasks during leave. If a genuine urgent issue arises, the manager should use the approved escalation channel and respect any boundaries established by People Operations.

## Return to Work and Extension Requests
People Operations confirms the expected return date and any approved transition or adjustment. If the employee expects to return later than planned, they should contact People Operations as early as possible; the employee should not rely only on an informal message to a teammate. People Operations explains whether updated information is required and how attendance should be recorded while a request is reviewed.

A return-to-work plan may include a phased schedule or temporary adjustment when appropriate. The manager should receive only the information needed to coordinate work. The employee's access, equipment, and work assignments should be restored or adjusted through approved systems. An employee should report any access issue through the service desk and should not use another person's account.

## Escalation and Recordkeeping
Questions about eligibility, statutory rights, pay, benefits, or a denied request must be directed to People Operations or the appropriate plan administrator. The assistant can provide policy references and general next steps but cannot determine legal rights or make a leave decision. HR records should be accurate, access-controlled, and retained according to the approved retention schedule. Managers should not keep informal medical files or request sensitive records by ordinary email.
